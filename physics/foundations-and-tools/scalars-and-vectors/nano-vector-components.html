Vector components (2D & 3D)

Step 1
Orient (what “components” really mean)
Free
Components are not “pieces” of the vector—components are the vector’s shadows (projections) onto chosen axes. Your job is to pick axes, define your angle convention, and keep signs consistent.
Tip: Decide whether angles are measured from +x, +y, or from another vector—then never change mid-problem.
Visible to everyone
Step 2
Understand (2D components with sign discipline)
Free
In 2D, a vector of magnitude \(A\) at angle \(\theta\) from +x has \[ A_x = A\cos\theta,\quad A_y = A\sin\theta \] **only if** \(\theta\) is measured from +x toward +y. If the problem uses a different reference angle, convert it first or use geometry with quadrant checks.
Exam trap: using \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) swapped because the angle is measured from +y.
Visible to everyone
Step 3
Apply (unit-vector form and reconstruction)
Free
Write vectors using unit vectors: \[ \vec{A}=A_x\,\hat{\imath}+A_y\,\hat{\jmath}\quad(\text{and }+A_z\,\hat{k}\text{ in 3D}) \] Rebuild magnitude and direction from components: \[ |\vec{A}|=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2},\quad \theta=\operatorname{atan2}(A_y,A_x) \] Using \(\operatorname{atan2}\) keeps the quadrant correct.
Shortcut: check signs by sketching the vector; components must point into the same quadrant.
