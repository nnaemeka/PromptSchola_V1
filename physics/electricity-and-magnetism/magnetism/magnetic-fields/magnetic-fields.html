Definition
Magnetic field definition
The magnetic field is a vector field that determines the magnetic force on moving charges and currents. It is defined operationally by how it acts, not by how it looks.
  • B as a vector field in space
  • Defined via force on moving charges
  • Units: tesla (T)
  • Direction: where it points matters

Field lines
Magnetic field lines
Field lines visualize the direction and relative strength of B. The tangent direction indicates B, and line density reflects magnitude.
  • Tangent gives direction of B
  • Line density indicates strength
  • Lines do not cross
  • Magnetic field lines form closed loops

Sources
Sources of magnetic fields
Magnetic fields are produced by moving charges (currents) and by intrinsic magnetic moments in matter. At the macroscopic level, currents and magnets are the main sources.
  • Currents in wires and loops
  • Permanent magnets (aligned moments)
  • Microscopic origins (electrons in atoms)
  • Earth’s magnetic field (conceptual source)
Start nano-lesson Most B fields trace back to moving charge.
Compare
Comparison with electric fields
Electric fields act on charges whether they move or not. Magnetic forces require motion and are perpendicular to velocity, so they change direction but not speed.
  • E acts on charges at rest; B does not
  • Magnetic force is perpendicular to velocity
  • B-field lines form closed loops (no monopoles)
  • Magnetic forces do no work on a point charge
