Magnetic Forces on Charges and Currents

Direction and geometry control everything: define vectors first, then compute magnitudes.

Core law
Lorentz force on moving charges
A charge moving in a magnetic field experiences a force perpendicular to its velocity and the field. The force depends on the component of velocity perpendicular to B.
  • Force magnitude depends on speed and B
  • Zero force if v is parallel to B
  • Force direction from a cross product rule
  • Implication: magnetic force bends trajectories

Currents
Force on a current-carrying wire
A current in a magnetic field experiences a force because many charges are moving through the conductor. The direction depends on current direction and field direction.
  • Force increases with current and wire length
  • Use conventional current direction
  • Perpendicular geometry gives maximum force
  • Basis for electromagnetic devices

Tool
Right-hand rules
Right-hand rules encode the cross product direction. Consistency matters: define v or I, define B, then determine the force direction.
  • v × B for a positive charge
  • I × B for a wire segment
  • Negative charge flips the direction
  • Common errors: mixing electron flow with I

Application
Applications to motors
Motors use magnetic forces on currents to produce torque and rotation. The core idea is a current loop in a magnetic field experiencing forces that form a couple.
  • Force pair produces a torque
  • Why a coil keeps rotating (conceptual)
  • Energy conversion: electrical to mechanical
  • Direction control by current reversal
