Ampère’s Law

Use when symmetry makes B constant along a chosen loop. Otherwise, Biot–Savart may be more direct.

Statement
Statement of Ampère’s law
Ampère’s law relates the circulation of the magnetic field around a closed loop to the current passing through the loop.
  • Closed-loop integral viewpoint
  • Enclosed current is what matters
  • Direction follows right-hand orientation
  • Most useful for steady currents

Symmetry
Symmetry and current loops
The power of Ampère’s law comes from choosing an Amperian loop where B has constant magnitude and direction along key segments.
  • Identify symmetry of the current distribution
  • Choose loop aligned with symmetry
  • Use segments where B is constant
  • Argue where dot products vanish

Applications
Solenoids and toroids
In ideal models, solenoids produce nearly uniform interior fields and toroids confine B to the core region. Ampère’s law captures both cleanly.
  • Solenoid: strong, nearly uniform interior B
  • Toroid: B concentrated inside the ring
  • Dependence on turns per length (conceptual)
  • Field direction from winding direction

Limitations
When Ampère’s law is not enough
Ampère’s law is always true for steady currents, but it is not always useful for finding B. If symmetry does not make B constant, the loop integral does not simplify.
  • Low-symmetry geometries are hard
  • Biot–Savart may be more practical
  • Know what “enclosed current” means
  • Recognize idealizations vs real devices
