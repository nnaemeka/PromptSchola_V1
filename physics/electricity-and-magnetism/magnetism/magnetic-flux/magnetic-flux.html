Magnetic Flux

Learn how flux depends on field strength, surface area, and orientation—then see how “changing flux” becomes the trigger for induced emf.

Definition
Definition of magnetic flux
Magnetic flux quantifies how much magnetic field passes through a surface. It is built from a dot product between the field and an area vector.
  • Area vector is perpendicular to the surface
  • Orientation controls the sign and magnitude
  • Flux is largest when surface is “facing” B
  • Units: weber (Wb) in SI

Surfaces
Flux through surfaces
For flat surfaces in uniform fields, flux depends on the angle between B and the surface normal. For curved surfaces, flux is the sum over small patches.
  • Flat surface: effective area concept
  • Angle is measured to the normal vector
  • Curved surfaces: add contributions patch-by-patch
  • Open vs closed surface idea
Start nano-lesson Angle is always relative to the normal.
Change
Changing flux concepts
Flux can change by changing B, changing the surface area, or changing orientation. These are the three basic routes to induction.
  • Change field magnitude (time-varying B)
  • Change area (move/reshape the loop)
  • Change angle (rotate the loop)
  • All lead into Faraday’s law
