Faraday’s Law of Electromagnetic Induction

Understand induced emf as a response to changing flux—then connect the idea to time-varying fields and generators.

Core law
Induced emf
Faraday’s law states that an emf is induced when the magnetic flux through a loop changes. The induced emf drives charges around the circuit.
  • Flux change is the trigger
  • Emf is around a closed path
  • Direction handled by Lenz’s law (next topic)
  • Works for moving loops or changing fields

Fields
Time-varying magnetic fields
A changing magnetic field can induce an electric field, producing emf even without a physical conductor moving through the field.
  • Changing B can induce circulating E
  • Induction does not require a battery
  • Loop geometry matters through flux
  • Preview of Maxwell’s unification idea

Application
Applications: generators
Generators convert mechanical rotation into electrical energy by changing the magnetic flux through a coil over time.
  • Rotating loop changes orientation → changing flux
  • Emf varies in time (often sinusoidal)
  • Energy source is mechanical work
  • Real devices add coils and magnetic cores

Modeling
What counts as “changing flux”
Flux can change by changing B, changing the loop area, or changing orientation. Recognizing the mechanism is often the main physics step.
  • Change field strength
  • Change loop area
  • Change loop orientation
  • All three are common in real devices
