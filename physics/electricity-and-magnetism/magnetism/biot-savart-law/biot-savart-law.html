Biot–Savart builds magnetic fields from current elements.

Treat a current as many tiny pieces, add their contributions, and use symmetry when possible.

This topic

Biot–Savart Law

Learn what the law says, how to apply it to simple geometries, and when Ampère’s law is a better tool.

Statement
Mathematical statement
The Biot–Savart law gives the magnetic field produced by a steady current as a sum (integral) of contributions from small current elements.
  • Current element produces a small dB
  • Direction is perpendicular to element and displacement
  • Magnitude decreases with distance squared
  • Built on superposition

Method
How to apply Biot–Savart
Application is systematic: define geometry, choose a coordinate, write the current element, and integrate. Symmetry can simplify direction and cancellation.
  • Sketch geometry and observation point
  • Express dℓ and r vectors
  • Use symmetry to reduce components
  • Integrate over the current path

Examples
Simple current geometries
Many standard results come from Biot–Savart: the field on the axis of a loop, near a long straight wire, and at the center of an arc.
  • Field due to a circular loop (axis)
  • Field at the center of a loop/arc
  • Connections to wire and solenoid results
  • Use symmetry to simplify integrals

Compare
Comparison with Ampère’s law
Biot–Savart always works for steady currents, but it can be algebra-heavy. Ampère’s law can be much faster when symmetry is high.
  • Biot–Savart: general but often longer
  • Ampère: powerful with symmetry
  • Choose the tool that fits the geometry
  • Both rely on superposition ideas
